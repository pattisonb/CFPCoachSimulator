What the numbers say

Crowds are sticky. A program's crowd is mostly its base: who it is, where it is, how big the stadium is. Winning moves it at the margin. Going from 5-7 to 8-4 and staying there lifts the crowd about 8 to 9 percent over three seasons; one good year alone moves it about 6 percent. The year-to-year change in attendance against the year-to-year change in winning has a correlation of only 0.08.

The Power 4 is capped by the building. With a median fill of 95 percent, most Power 4 programs cannot grow their crowd much however well they play. Texas A&M, Nebraska and Colorado are above 100 percent of listed capacity. Their growth with success shows up on TV and in NIL, which is why the game's market metric has a TV half.

The Group of 5 has room. At a 60 percent median fill, a Group of 5 program's crowd can grow by half. The biggest ten-year risers are there: North Texas (+80%), SMU (+59%, now a Power 4 program), UNLV (+59%), Georgia State (+53%), App State (+48%). Eastern Michigan's +203% is a reporting change rather than a crowd.

Losing costs more slowly than winning pays. Northwestern (-65%, a stadium rebuild), Temple (-60%), Akron (-57%), UCLA (-44%) and Stanford (-44%) all lost close to half their crowd over ten years of losing or of a market drifting away. Nebraska is the counter-example: 86,500 a game through nine straight losing seasons, because the base is the deepest in the sport.

A new coach is the fastest lift. Colorado went from 42,800 to 53,200 in Deion Sanders' first season and has stayed at capacity. Indiana went from 44,800 to 48,400 to 51,200 in Curt Cignetti's two seasons. Both arrived with a story as well as wins, which is why the game lets a hire itself count a little.

What the game will do with this. The market score keeps a fixed base from these numbers and a live part that moves each offseason by about a third of the three-year winning change, weighted two to one toward last season, capped by capacity on the crowd side and open on the TV side. A decade of winning lifts a program about 15 points; a decade of losing takes about 10.
