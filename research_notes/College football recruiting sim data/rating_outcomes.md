# Recruiting Star Ratings vs. Player Outcomes (for recruit potential/development parameters)

Scope: FBS high school recruits, 2002–2026 classes. Research done October 2026. Several primary pages (On3, MGoBlog) returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool; figures from them are taken from search-result summaries and are marked "(via search summary)". Treat those as less verified.

## NFL draft rates by star rating (overall and first round)

### Takeaway
Across studies, services and years the draft rates are very stable: 5-stars about 60–69% drafted, 4-stars about 22–26%, 3-stars about 5–6.5%, 2-stars about 0.5–1.4% (rates for 2-star-or-lower vary widely with the denominator used). First-round rates are about 1 in 3 for 5-stars, about 4% for 4-stars, and under 0.5% for 3-stars. Even so, 3-stars are the largest group of drafted players, because there are so many more of them.

### Cited Findings
**Draft rate by star (multiple cohorts):**
- 247Sports Composite, 2013 class to 2017 draft: 5-star 35 players, 65.7% drafted; 4-star 330, 23.0%; 3-star 1,790, 5.0%; 2-star 1,720, 1.4%. 2014 class to 2018 draft: 5-star 33, 57.6%; 4-star 296, 23.6%; 3-star 1,541, 6.9%; 2-star 1,666, 1.1%. The two-year simple averages are 61.6% / 23.3% / 5.95% / 1.25%. — [247Sports, "How recruiting rankings fare projecting future NFL Draft picks" (c. 2018)](https://247sports.com/article/nfl-draft-recruiting-rankings-go-hand-in-hand--117819292/)
- 2016–17 class to 2020 draft (Preston Guy via 247Sports): 5-star 19 of 30 drafted (stated as 62.3%; the arithmetic gives 63.3%), first-round rate 34.3%. 4-star 73 of 311 (23.5%), first round 3.8%. 3-star 110 of 1,831 (6%), first round 0.4% (8 of 1,831). 2-star 1,949 players, first-round rate 0.005% (the article's bullet summary conflicts with its transcript). — [247Sports Arkansas, "Nationally, three-star recruits have modest chance for greatness"](https://247sports.com/college/arkansas/article/three-star-recruits-and-nfl-draft-233182492/)
- Average across four draft classes since 2010 (years not named): 5-star 61% (about 20 of about 33 per class; the average drafted 5-star went in Round 2); 4-star 23% (about 78 of about 332; average Round 3); 3-star 5.1% (about 106 of about 2,087; average Round 4); below 3-star 0.51% (about 51 of about 10,044). 39% of 5-stars were not drafted. Blue-chips are under 4% of players but 38% of draftees. — [Last Word on College Football (2021)](https://lastwordonsports.com/collegefootball/2021/07/12/what-do-those-star-ratings-mean/)
- On3, multi-year analysis (2022, covering five drafts): 63% 5-star draft rate and 5.3% 3-star draft rate; 4-stars about 5.2x as likely to be drafted as 2-star-or-lower (via search summary). — [On3, "On3 data reveals 5-star recruits have 63% draft rate"](https://www.on3.com/news/on3-data-reveals-5-star-recruits-have-63-draft-rate)
- On3 single drafts, by On3 Industry Ranking (via search summary):
  - 2023: 5-star about 81.3%, 4-star 22.9%, 3-star 6.3%. — [On3 2023 Draft by Stars](https://www.on3.com/nfl/draft/2023/draft-by-stars/)
  - 2024: 5-star 43.8% (14 drafted), 4-star 22.0%, 3-star 6.5%. — [On3 2024 Draft by Stars](https://www.on3.com/nfl/draft/2024/draft-by-stars)
  - 2025: 5-star 22 drafted (68.8%), 4-star 97 (26.3%), 3-star 110 (5.0%), 2-star-or-lower 28 (stated as 4.3%). 5-stars were 0.7% of recruits but 8.6% of draftees; 3-stars were 42.8% of draftees. — [On3 2025 Draft by Stars](https://www.on3.com/nfl/draft/2025/draft-by-stars/)
  - Note: On3's 2-star-or-lower rate of 4.3% is far above every other source (about 0.5–1.4%). It probably uses only players in On3's rated database as the denominator, so it should not be used.
- Long-run ESPN data, 2000–2024: 5-star about 65% drafted; top-15 5-stars about 73%; 5-stars ranked 16–30 about 50% drafted in Round 7 or not at all; 4-star about 22–25%; 3-star about 5%. First-round rates: 5-star about 22%, 4-star about 4%, 2-star under 1%. **Caveat: the author says these statistics were generated with Google Gemini and not verified. Low reliability; use only as corroboration.** — [Eisenmann Substack, "From Friday Night Lites to the NFL Draft"](https://joeeisenmann.substack.com/p/from-friday-night-lites-to-the-nfl)
- Rivals 2002–2009 (2,410 four-stars, 279 five-stars): 5-stars "only made the NFL twice as often as four-stars" (this counts making an NFL roster, not the draft). — [BuzzFeed News, "Short, Fat Guys Are Overrated"](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/jakel11/short-fat-guys-are-overrated-and-other-observations-from-a-b)
- CFB Data Lab, a decade of classes, Power 5 signees: 41% of 5-stars appeared on an NFL roster at least once, versus 10% of 2-stars. — [FootballScoop, "Do stars matter in recruiting?"](https://footballscoop.com/news/stars-matter-recruiting-data-says)

**Share of draft picks by star:**
- 2018 draft (254 picks): 5-star 19, 4-star 70, 3-star 106, 2-star 19, NR 42, so 65.7% were 3-star or lower. 2017 draft: 5-star 23, 4-star 76, 3-star 90, 2-star 25, NR 38. 14 of 32 first-round picks in 2018 were 3-star or lower. — [247Sports (c. 2018)](https://247sports.com/article/nfl-draft-recruiting-rankings-go-hand-in-hand--117819292/)
- 2026 draft, first round: 25 of 32 (78%) were rated 4- or 5-star by at least one service; 1 of the 7 non-blue-chips signed with a G6 school out of high school (via search summary). — [On3/Rivals, "How the recruiting services fared in the 2026 NFL Draft's first round"](https://www.on3.com/rivals/news/how-the-recruiting-services-fared-in-the-2026-nfl-drafts-first-round/)

### Inferences
- Suggested sim targets (estimates synthesized from the above): P(drafted) by star is 5★ about 62%, 4★ about 23%, 3★ about 5.5%, 2★ about 1%, unrated about 0.3–0.5%. P(first round) is 5★ about 25–34%, 4★ about 4%, 3★ about 0.4%, 2★ about 0.05% or less.
- Ratio checks: a 5★ is about 2.7x as likely to be drafted as a 4★, which is about 4–5x a 3★, which is about 5–10x a 2★.
- Draft composition the sim should reproduce (estimate, from the 2017, 2018 and 2025 counts): about 8–9% 5★, 28–30% 4★, 38–43% 3★, and 20–25% 2★/NR/JUCO/other. In the first round, roughly 55–78% blue-chip.
- Year-to-year noise is large for 5-stars (On3: 43.8% in 2024 vs. 81.3% in 2023), because only about 32 players per class are 5-stars.

### Gaps
- No full seven-round 2026 draft breakdown by star was found, and On3's 2026 page returned 403.
- First-round rates by star come from essentially one cohort (2016–17 class) plus the unverified AI-generated series.

## Starting, all-conference and All-America rates by star rating

### Takeaway
Hard national data on starter or all-conference rates by star is scarce. The best All-America data (Rivals, 2008–2012) gives odds of about 1 in 4 for 5-stars, 1 in 16 for 4-stars, 1 in 56 for 3-stars and 1 in 127 for 2-stars. Even so, more than half of All-Americans were 3-star or lower.

### Cited Findings
- Rivals 2008–2012 classes, 316 players with at least one major All-America selection: 42 (about 13%) were 5-stars, 81 (about 25%) were Rivals top-100, and 162 (more than half) were 3-star or worse. Odds of becoming an All-American: 5★ 1 in 4, top-100 1 in 6, 4★ 1 in 16, 3★ 1 in 56, 2★ 1 in 127, all FBS signees 1 in 45. 2- and 3-stars are more than 86% of signees. — [Matt Hinton, CBS Sports, "Recruiting by the numbers," Feb 4, 2013](https://www.cbssports.com/college-football/news/recruiting-by-the-numbers-why-the-sites-get-the-rankings-right)
- 247Sports Composite, roughly the 2004–2014 era: by a subjective "lived up to the hype / became a star" standard, only 97 5-stars, or 33.8% ("barely more than one in three"), qualified. The author notes the 2013–14 classes were still incomplete. By position: QBs 9 of 23, DEs 11 of 31. — [Saturday Down South, "5-star recruits of the last decade"](https://www.saturdaydownsouth.com/sec-football/5-star-recruits-last-decade/)
- In Arkansas program history, 2-stars make up 9.7% of players but only 2.4% of All-American honors (single program, via search summary). — [247Sports Arkansas](https://247sports.com/college/arkansas/article/three-star-recruits-and-nfl-draft-233182492/)
- Team-level validity: Rivals/Scout total points and average stars explained up to 45% of the variance in end-of-season Sagarin ratings and wins (2002–2006 classes). — [Herda et al., JQAS 2009](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bpj/jqsprt/v5y2009i4n4.html)
- 247 ratings explained up to 36% of the variance in Sagarin ratings. — [UNCA Mathematics and Sports paper](https://libjournals.unca.edu/OJS/index.php/mas/article/view/13)
- McClendon & Nadrowski (2021): three star tiers were no more predictive of team success than two tiers (2016–2019). — [Janeway / UNC Press, Mathematics and Sports](https://janeway.uncpress.org/ms/article/id/1223)

### Inferences
- Implied All-America share by star (estimate): 5★ about 13% of All-Americans, 4★ about 30–35% (the residual, since 4★ was not stated), 3★ and below about 51%.
- All-conference rates (estimates only, not sourced): first or second team all-conference is roughly 2–4x as common as All-America. A plausible scaling is 5★ about 55–65%, 4★ about 25–30%, 3★ about 8–10%, 2★ about 3–4%. Calibrate in the sim so that each conference's all-conference teams have a blue-chip share consistent with conference talent.
- Multi-year starter rates (estimates only): for 5-stars, the "about one-third become stars" and "about 61% drafted" figures imply that most become starters at some point (likely 75–85%). For 3-stars at P4 programs, perhaps 35–50% start at least one season. These are not sourced figures.

### Gaps
- No national study found that tabulates multi-year starter or all-conference rates by star rating. CollegeFootballData.com has the raw data (recruits plus player usage) but no published table was found.
- No modern (2015+) All-America breakdown by star using the 247 Composite was found.

## Busts and breakouts

### Takeaway
About 35–40% of 5-stars are never drafted, and only about one-third are judged to have fully lived up to the hype. On the other side, 3-star-or-lower recruits make up about 60–65% of all draftees, more than half of All-Americans (2008–12), and 7–14 of 32 first-round picks in recent drafts.

### Cited Findings
- 39% of 5-stars were not drafted. — [Last Word on College Football](https://lastwordonsports.com/collegefootball/2021/07/12/what-do-those-star-ratings-mean/)
- 33.8% of the 5-stars studied "lived up to the hype." — [Saturday Down South](https://www.saturdaydownsouth.com/sec-football/5-star-recruits-last-decade/)
- Rivals 2002–09 five-star DEs: 12 of 31 (38.7%) made the NFL, the lowest of any 5-star position, and none made a Pro Bowl. All 5 five-star TEs made the NFL. More than 30% of 4- and 5-star TEs went pro (the highest position group), while QBs had the lowest pro rate. — [BuzzFeed News](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/jakel11/short-fat-guys-are-overrated-and-other-observations-from-a-b)
- Five-star skill players (QB/RB/WR): about 58% not drafted (**AI-generated, unverified**). — [Eisenmann Substack](https://joeeisenmann.substack.com/p/from-friday-night-lites-to-the-nfl)
- Recent share of 3-star-or-lower: 65.7% of 2018 draftees, and 14 of 32 first-rounders in 2018. — [247Sports](https://247sports.com/article/nfl-draft-recruiting-rankings-go-hand-in-hand--117819292/)
- In 2026, 7 of 32 first-rounders were rated 3-star or lower by every service (via search summary). — [On3/Rivals 2026](https://www.on3.com/rivals/news/how-the-recruiting-services-fared-in-the-2026-nfl-drafts-first-round/)
- Example breakout: Baker Mayfield was ranked No. 1,029 in the 2013 class. — [247Sports](https://247sports.com/article/nfl-draft-recruiting-rankings-go-hand-in-hand--117819292/)

### Inferences
- Sim design (estimates): model the potential spread around star rating as wide and right-skewed for low-star players. Breakout probability per individual is low (about 5% of 3★ drafted, about 1% of 2★), but in aggregate low-star players produce the majority of stars.
- Suggested 5★ outcome mix (estimate): about 33% "star" (All-America or first-round level), about 30% solid multi-year starter or mid-round pick, about 20% role player, about 15–20% bust or transfer-out with little impact.

### Gaps
- No systematic "5-stars who never started" rate was found.

## Transfer rates by star rating

### Takeaway
About 25% of 247-rated recruits from the 2016–2021 classes entered the portal by early 2022, and about 4–6% of a class transfers each season. 4-stars transfer most (about 35%), then 5-stars (about 27%) and 3-stars (about 22%).

### Cited Findings
- 247Sports 2016–2021 classes, 16,964 rated recruits, portal tracked 2019 through February 1, 2022: 4,174 (24.6%) entered the portal and 2,743 (16.2%) transferred away from their original school. About 4–6% of a class transfers each season. Higher-rated recruits transfer more, except that 5-stars transfer less than 4-stars. Transfer rates plateau at about 25% for class scores above 250, and classes of 25 or more players show well-above-average rates. — [Bud Davis, CFB Numbers Substack](https://cfbnumbers.substack.com/p/statistical-properties-of-the-cfb)
- Portal transfer rates by star: 4★ 35%, 5★ 27%, 3★ 22%. Little rating difference between transfers and non-transfers among 4- and 5-stars (via search summary; page returned 403). — [MGoBlog, "A deep data dive into the CFB transfer portal"](https://mgoblog.com/diaries/deep-data-dive-cfb-transfer-portal)
- Blue-chip QBs transfer at an "alarming rate" (headline only; not quantified here). — [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/sports/why-blue-chip-quarterback-recruits-are-transferring-at-an-alarming-rate)

### Inferences
- The 2016–21 data predates the 2021 one-time transfer rule and NIL, so it is likely an undercount for post-2021 classes. Sim estimate for current era: about 8–12% of a class enters the portal each year, and about 35–50% of a class transfers at least once over 4–5 years (estimate, not sourced).
- Shape of the curve: 4★ highest, because they are blocked behind 5★ players at elite programs, while 5★ players are usually starting.

### Gaps
- No post-2023 portal-rate-by-star study was found. A 2–3-year cumulative rate by star was not available.

## Redshirt and development timelines (incl. position)

### Takeaway
Only a minority of even top-50 freshmen start in year 1. Among the top-50 true-freshman OL in each class, only 7–10 earned a start, and about 70% redshirted (2021). OL typically start around year 3 (redshirt sophomore), while DBs and blue-chip QBs get the most early playing time.

### Cited Findings
- Top-50 true-freshman OL (Phil Steele), combined starts / games played: 2020 23/142 (8 made a start); 2021 58/192 (9 started, 5 started 8+ games, about 70% redshirted); 2022 64/181 (7 started, 4 full-season starters); 2023 75/182; 2024 25/136; 2025 64/211 (10 started). — [Phil Steele](https://philsteele.com/?p=523814)
- Coaches are more likely to fill an OL hole with a transfer than a true freshman. — [Phil Steele (search summary)](https://philsteele.com/how-many-of-the-top-50-true-frosh-ols-have-played-the-last-four-years/)
- Top-50 true-freshman DBs combined to start 72 games, 16 of the 50 earned at least one start, and DBs had the highest playing-time share of true freshmen in 2020 (via search summary). — [Phil Steele DBs](https://philsteele.com/how-many-true-frosh-dbs-have-played-the-last-two-years￼/)
- SEC true-freshman QBs: average games started rose from 8.8 (2006–2013 per period) to 24.3 in the most recent four seasons. 4- and 5-stars started 120 of 167 true-freshman QB starts (72%). — [HawgBeat](https://arkansas.rivals.com/news/hawgbeat-study-how-sec-true-freshman-qbs-have-fared)
- Rivals 2002–09: OL were 15.5% of ranked recruits but 18.1% of NFL roster spots (over-represented in the NFL), while QBs were 6.7% vs. 4.5% (under-represented). — [BuzzFeed News](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/jakel11/short-fat-guys-are-overrated-and-other-observations-from-a-b)

### Inferences
- Sim timelines (estimates):
  - Typical first-start year by star: 5★ year 1–2, 4★ year 2–3, 3★ year 3–4 at P4 (earlier at G5), 2★ year 3–5.
  - By position: OL/DL about 1 year later than average; DB/WR/RB about 0.5 year earlier; QB depends heavily on the depth chart.
- Only about 14–20% of top-50 freshman OL start any game in year 1, which implies at most a few percent of all freshman OL do (estimate).

### Gaps
- No national dataset of first-start year by star and position was found. CollegeFootballData.com's usage and roster data could produce one.

## Does the ranking service matter? Predictiveness within a star band

### Takeaway
Services agree on most top prospects, and differences in predictive accuracy between them are small. Rating within a band matters a lot: top-15 5-stars are drafted far more often than 5-stars ranked 16–30, and the top-100 / All-America odds step up sharply.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 first round: On3 was closest to draft slot for 8 first-rounders, while 247Sports, ESPN and Rivals tied at 6 each (via search summary). On3 is the publisher and an interested party. — [On3/Rivals 2026](https://www.on3.com/rivals/news/how-the-recruiting-services-fared-in-the-2026-nfl-drafts-first-round/)
- On3 also claims superior 2025 draft-prediction accuracy (self-reported; page not fetched). — [On3, "2025 draft data reveals On3's strength"](https://www.on3.com/news/on3-nfl-draft-ranking-accuracy-2025)
- Within the 5★ band, 2011–2016: top-15 ranked players had a 62.1% "hit rate" and lower-ranked 5-stars did much worse (via search summary of a cited source). — [Search summary referencing 247/On3 studies](https://www.si.com/college/notredame/recruiting/five-star-recruits-and-the-nfl-draft)
- ESPN data: top-15 5-stars about 73% drafted versus about 50% of 5-stars ranked 16–30 going in Round 7 or undrafted (**AI-generated, unverified**). — [Eisenmann Substack](https://joeeisenmann.substack.com/p/from-friday-night-lites-to-the-nfl)
- Rivals top-100 have 1-in-6 All-America odds, between 5★ (1 in 4) and 4★ (1 in 16). — [CBS Sports 2013](https://www.cbssports.com/college-football/news/recruiting-by-the-numbers-why-the-sites-get-the-rankings-right)
- Rating scale: on a 100-point scale, 100–95 = 5★ and 94–90 = 4★. Each Top247 class has about 32 five-stars. — [FootballScoop](https://footballscoop.com/news/stars-matter-recruiting-data-says); [247Sports](https://247sports.com/article/nfl-draft-recruiting-rankings-go-hand-in-hand--117819292/)
- Three star tiers add little beyond two tiers in predicting team success. — [Janeway / UNC Press](https://janeway.uncpress.org/ms/article/id/1223)

### Inferences
- In the sim, use a continuous composite rating as the driver of expected potential, with stars as display labels. Outcome probability should rise steeply (roughly exponentially) at the very top, e.g. top-10 overall vs. 5★ #25 vs. 4★ #100.
- Service choice: a single "industry composite" is sufficient. Modeling disagreement between services adds flavor but has negligible predictive value per the available evidence.

### Gaps
- The "62.1% top-15 hit rate" figure was seen only in a search summary; the original article was not verified.
- No independent, peer-reviewed comparison of 247 vs. On3 vs. ESPN predictive validity for individual outcomes was found.
